    02  How it works

    It reads for shape, not for truth.

    Persuasion leans on familiar wording. Borrowed agreement. Experts with no names. A deadline. A doubter made to look foolish. BiasClear looks for that wording, outlines each match, and tells you what the move is called.

    It does not decide whether a claim is right. It does not score the text or the writer. It shows the build, and leaves the reading to you.

    1. You paste text. It never leaves this tab.
    2. Twenty rules from the source on GitHub look for known moves. This page skips the source’s citation check, so it can mark a little more.
    3. Each move is outlined, named, and sorted into one of three tiers.
    4. You decide what it means. Nothing is scored.

    Tier I

    Ideological

    What you are told is settled. These moves make a claim feel agreed, proven or decided before you have weighed it, set unequal sides as even, or answer a worry with a promise.

    Moves in this tier

    • Consensus as proof
    • Unsourced authority
    • Dissent dismissed
    • Inevitability
    • Appeal to tradition
    • False equivalence
    • Soft consensus
    • Aspirational deflection

    Tier II

    Psychological

    How you are pushed. These moves put fear, shame, pride, decency or a deadline where a reason should be, cut the choices to two or the causes to one, or wave off the doubter.

    Moves in this tier

    • False binary
    • Fear + urgency
    • Shame lever
    • Feeling for evidence
    • Moral high ground
    • Competence dismissed
    • Dismissal by reframing
    • Single cause
    • One culprit

    Tier III

    Institutional

    Whose voice it borrows, and how large it makes things. These moves lean on unnamed bodies, standards and process language to sound official, or call a harm total and final.

    Moves in this tier

    • Fog language
    • Vague authority
    • Total harm

    03  What it misses

    What it catches, and what it walks past.

    BiasClear knows the usual wording of a move. When the same move is made in other words, it can miss it. Here are two of the texts from above, read by the same rules, with the misses marked by hand.

            On measurement

            These rules have not yet been measured on a public benchmark. Read the marks as prompts to look closer, not as findings.

            Figures published earlier were measured on the same texts the rules were tuned on, so they said nothing about new text, and have been withdrawn. Per-rule precision and recall on SemEval-2020 Task 11 and SemEval-2023 Task 3 will come from a script in the repo. Until then this page quotes no figure.

            04  Privacy

            Your text stays in this tab.

            No uploads.

            The rules run inside this page. What you paste is read here and sent nowhere.

            No cookies.

            The page sets none. It keeps no record of what you paste, and forgets it when you close the tab.

            No accounts.

            Nothing to sign up for. Nothing to sign in to. Nothing to hand over.

            Check it yourself.

            When it opens, the page loads its two typefaces from Google Fonts, in full. After that it asks for nothing. The counter under the text starts once both typefaces are in, then counts every request your browser reports. Paste something and watch it stay at 0, or look at this page’s requests in your browser’s network panel. Browser extensions can make requests of their own; the counter may show some of those too.

            Letters the typefaces do not include are drawn in your device’s own fonts.
